Experiment 0: Controlled downlink noise and link adaptation

Add noise to the downlink, watch the UE report a lower channel quality and the gNB choose a lower MCS, and compare the goodput with the prediction from the MCS. Reading: Training Module 0. The lecturer or assistant runs the bench (unlock with the admin password in the toolbar); students observe and fill in their own tables.

* Predicted = k × η(MCS) × (1 − BLER), with η the spectral efficiency of the MCS (TS 38.214 Table 5.1.3.1-1, 64QAM table) and k = 15.9 M data resource elements per second for 106 PRB (scaled by PRB/106 for other carriers), measured in Experiment 0b. η and the prediction use the mean MCS of the 20 s window. Reference bench run (Experiment 0b): MCS 28 and 87.5 Mbit/s from about 24 dB SINR; link up down to about 4.3 dB.

Theory in short

  • The UE measures the downlink and reports a CQI (0–15): the highest modulation and code rate it could decode with a block error rate of about 10 % (TS 38.214).
  • The gNB maps the CQI to an MCS (0–28). A higher MCS packs more bits into each resource element: η = Qm × R, from 0.23 bit/RE at MCS 0 to 5.55 bit/RE at MCS 28.
  • Goodput follows the MCS: goodput ≈ k × η(MCS) × (1 − BLER). The constant k counts the resource elements per second that carry data after overhead (about 60 % of all DL resource elements on this bench).
  • On this bench the SINR falls by about 2 dB for each dB added to the noise setting (hypothesis from Experiment 0b: the setting acts on amplitude).

Discussion

  1. How do SINR, CQI and MCS change from Off to Poor? Is the change smooth or in steps, and why?
  2. How close is the measured goodput to the prediction from the MCS? Where does the difference come from?
  3. Compute the Shannon capacity B·log2(1 + SINR) for each preset. How many dB is the link from the Shannon limit?
  4. How does the BLER change from Off to Poor? What would happen with a fixed MCS instead of link adaptation (Experiment 5)?
  5. Why does TCP lose more than UDP when the channel gets worse?

* Theory: TS 38.306 §4.1.2 peak rate for 1 layer, 64QAM, Rmax = 948/1024, overhead 14 % DL / 8 % UL, times the TDD share of the slots (DL 104/140, UL 32/140). Goodput is MAC goodput reported by the gNB, in simulated time. DL loss of 5 % or more shows that the UDP test saturated the link. Reference bench run (Experiment 1, 14 Oct 2026): UDP DL 18.1 / 43.3 / 87.5 / 226.5 Mbit/s at 24 / 51 / 106 / 273 PRB.

Theory in short

  • At 30 kHz subcarrier spacing a carrier holds 24 PRB in 10 MHz, 51 in 20 MHz, 106 in 40 MHz and 273 in 100 MHz (TS 38.101-1). One PRB is 12 subcarriers.
  • Peak rate: Qm × Rmax × N_PRB × 12 × 28 000 symbols/s × (1 − OH), then times the share of DL or UL symbols in the TDD pattern.
  • Hypothesis to test: the rate grows in proportion to the PRB count, except that a small carrier loses a larger share to fixed overhead (SSB, CORESET 0).
  • The radio is simulated (rfsim): the channel is set in software and goodput is counted in simulated time, so it does not depend on how fast the PC runs.

Discussion

  1. Does the goodput grow in proportion to the bandwidth? Which bandwidth deviates, and why?
  2. What share of the theoretical rate is reached? Which overheads explain the gap?
  3. Compute the Shannon capacity from the measured SINR. Why is it far above the measured goodput?
  4. Compute the thermal noise power −174 dBm/Hz + 10·log10(B) for each bandwidth. What would a wider carrier mean for the SNR on a real radio?
  5. Why does the TCP test give a lower number than the UDP test?
  6. Compare the share of theory reached in the uplink and in the downlink. If the uplink share is larger, which downlink overheads does the uplink not carry?
